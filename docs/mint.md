# The mint's words

Generated by `kamanu assay --glossary docs/mint.md` from the one table in the code (`kamanu.assay.mint.TABLE`) that the printed hallmark, the registry, the Catalogue and the house rules page read. Each coin word means one exact thing and nothing else; no number or level changes with the words.

- **face value**: the cap: the most the limit is set to let through, in the policy's own units
- **assay**: the run that gives the ceiling: the most that can really be spent under the limit (lint's exposure on the scope ledger)
- **fineness**: face value over ceiling, shown as a millesimal mark with three decimals (.500, 1.000), never rounded up
- **fine**: fineness 1.000 at the hallmark's level, and only then
- **debased**: fineness below 1.000, shown with the amount by which it is: the ceiling less the face value, in the limit's own units (unbounded when the ceiling is)
- **stranded budget**: the most free headroom left when a request that would fit is refused (lint's worst Tight figure)
- **hallmark**: the signed record of one assay: its figures, levels, hypotheses and hashes
- **mint mark**: the hashes of the model (the policy and its lint form), the profile and the Lean sources
- **struck**: the date of the run that issued the hallmark
- **obverse**: the face of a hallmark: face value, fineness, stranded budget and level
- **reverse**: the back of a hallmark: the hypotheses, each proved, measured or assumed, and the trace
- **touchstone**: the coach: the product that checks a payment before it is made (does it fit, the most that fits, and when the full amount will)
- **countermark**: the connector assessment: the product that checks that no caller reaches another tenant's money or records through a connector
- **pyx**: the re-measurement of a hallmark, kept as signed entries beside it
- **trial of the pyx**: the launch: the hallmarks shown in public with their re-measurement
- **lapsed**: a hallmark whose re-measurement failed or ran out (past its valid-through date)

A page shows a hallmark's obverse with its reverse, whole or not at all. A limit is called debased beside its trace and its level, never without them (rule E1).
